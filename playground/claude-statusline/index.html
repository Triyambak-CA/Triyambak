Know before you run dry

Claude Code is the AI coding tool I run in my terminal. My plan gives it a 5-hour limit and a weekly one. Now two live meters under the prompt tell me how much I have used, and whether it will last until the refill.

Overview

What it shows

Three rows under the prompt. The first is the model, how full the conversation is, and the folder. The other two are my plan's limits.

Opus 5.5 │ ctx 12% │ ~/projects/invoice-parser main 5-hour ██████┃██▋░░░░░░ 60% runs dry at 1:20pm, 1h 40m before the 3:00pm refill weekly ████▊░░░┃░░░░░░░ 30% on track for about 54% by Mon 2:00pm
  • The bar fills with how much of the limit I have used.
  • The white post is the clock: how far through the window I am.
  • Fill past the post means I am spending faster than the limit refills.
  • The sentence says what that means: the share I will have used by the refill, or the time I would run dry, how early that is, and when it refills.
  • The colours follow: mint with room to spare, amber when I would end at 85% or more, coral when I would run dry first or am at the cap.

The idea

Pace, not just percent

A percentage alone says how much is gone, not whether the rest will last. The forecast compares two shares: how much of the limit I have used, and how much of the window has passed.

  • 60% used when 40% of the five hours has gone means spending 1.5 times faster than the limit allows.
  • At that rate it lands at 150% by the refill, so it runs dry first: at 1:20pm, 1 hour 40 minutes before the 3:00pm refill.
  • The forecast assumes I keep going at my average rate so far. A heavy first hour reads "runs dry" until the hours catch up, and in the first tenth of a window it says "too early to tell" instead.

Behind it

How it was made

Built with Claude Code. Claude Code already hands its status line the plan's usage figures; a small shell script of about 160 lines works out the pace and the forecast and prints the three rows in fixed colours. No app, no extra login, and it redraws after every reply and every minute. Twenty-five automated checks hold its rows, each forecast worked out by hand.
